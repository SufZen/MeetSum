01:00Principles

Five rules that settle arguments

Hebrew first, not Hebrew too

Every screen is designed in RTL with code-switched Hebrew and English, then checked in LTR. Logical properties only. Direction comes from the language you know.

Rule: a component isn't done until its Hebrew screenshot is.

Every AI claim has a source

Summary lines, decisions, actions and answers carry a timecode that plays the moment. Hover any line and its source lanes light up on the timeline. If a citation can't resolve, it isn't shown.

Rule: no citation, no claim.

The AI mark is a citation

Model-written text carries the cited mark and a hairline in the colours of the speakers it came from. When a person edits it, it becomes theirs and loses the mark. Iris is used for nothing else.

Rule: one AI hue, one mark, and the mark points at people.

Calm by default, expressive on meaning

Routine feedback is fast and quiet (70–160ms). Longer motion is kept for moments that change what you know: a summary arrives and its sources light up, a citation lands, recording starts.

Rule: if it moves for more than 240ms, it must explain something.

Honest state

Real durations, real progress, real owners. Show exceptions, not reassurance: no "Ready" on every row. Unconfirmed speaker names say so.

Rule: never invent a number to fill a layout.

02:00Foundations

Colour, type, space

All colour is OKLCH, generated from one neutral hue (≈242) and four reserved accents. Contrast ratios below are measured live against --surface in the current theme.

Semantic colour

Speakers

Eight hues at matched lightness, so no voice reads louder than another. They also mark provenance: the hairline beside AI text is split into the colours of the speakers it cites. Assigned in order of first speech and remembered per person across meetings.

Type

IBM Plex Sans, Sans Hebrew and Mono were drawn as one superfamily, so x-heights and weights match when a sentence switches script mid-line. Body is 14px; transcript reading is 16px. Hebrew gets its own line-heights through :lang(he) token overrides, no italics, no negative tracking, and labels switch from mono to the Hebrew face because Plex Mono has no Hebrew.

03:00Motion

Two speeds: productive and expressive

Productive motion (70–160ms) confirms that something happened. Expressive motion (240–400ms) is reserved for moments that change what you know. Springs move things; opacity and colour fade on their own curve and never bounce. With reduced motion, travel is removed and fades stay, so feedback never disappears.

    Set dir from the language you already know: ASR gives each segment a language, and summaries are written in the meeting's language. Use dir="auto" only for text of unknown language (search, chat input, user fields). It picks direction from the first strong character, so "Lisboa Riverside נכנסת…" would render left-to-right. Names and numbers inside sentences get <bdi>.

    What mirrors

    Mirrors
    Never mirrors

    Shown right to left, as in a Hebrew screen. Media controls, the scrubber, the speaker timeline and the recording waveform show time, not reading direction, so they stay left-to-right in Hebrew. Give icons that mirror data-icon="directional"; everything else is static.

    Accessibility floor

    • Text 4.5:1, controls and input boundaries 3:1, in both themes (measured in Foundations).
    • Icon buttons are 28px. Citation chips are 20px tall and spaced so a 24px circle never overlaps a neighbour (WCAG 2.5.8 spacing exception). Timeline markers are a shortcut: every moment they mark is also reachable from the summary and the scrubber.
    • The scrubber is a keyboard slider: arrows ±5s, Page Up/Down ±30s, Home/End, Space plays.
    • Focus rings use a transparent outline plus a shadow, so they survive Windows forced-colours mode.
    • Live regions announce once: "Recording started", never the ticking timer. Streams announce on completion.
    • Every label and aria-label is translated, including icon buttons.

    AI UX contract

    • Citations resolve to a real segment or they aren't rendered.
    • Stop is visible for the whole stream; Regenerate is one click.
    • Feedback is a flag on the exact line, so it can be checked against the transcript. No thumbs.
    • Edited AI text becomes human text and survives reprocessing.
    • Unknown answers say so plainly. The scope (this meeting or all) is always shown.
